1. **“The ten-evening pilot measured 63 visits in 13 hours and proved the added hour caused an 18% increase.” — Partially supported.** The register supports 63 visits in 13 observed staffed hours across **nine** of the ten scheduled evenings. S10’s two hours have no usable count. The 18% causal claim is **unsupported**. The press release shows that the Board described the pilot as causal proof. Separately, the vendor asserted an 18% increase for evening programs without supplying data or attributing the figure to this pilot. The pilot had no control group, randomized schedule, or comparable pre-pilot evening series from which to estimate an increase. (`LIB-REGISTER`, rows `S01`–`S10`; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §§1–3; `LIB-PRESS` §§1–2; `LIB-VENDOR` §1)

2. **“The press release is an independent replication of the analyst’s result.” — Unsupported.** The release explicitly credits the analyst summary for its figures and reports no new measurement. The register, summary, and release trace back to the same nine observed evenings. The release establishes what the Board reported. The register is what verifies the count. (`LIB-PRESS` §§1–2; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §1; `LIB-REGISTER`, rows `S01`–`S09`)

3. **“The pilot outperformed the network by 21.2%.” — Misleading.** Both rates and the arithmetic are supported. The observed pilot rate was 63/13, or 4.85 rounded. The network rate was 1,200/300, or 4.00. Using the unrounded pilot rate gives a difference of 21.2% relative to the network rate. “Outperformed” implies a comparable benchmark, but the network row covers **all staffed hours at four branches throughout 2025**, while the pilot rate covers **nine observed evenings in October 2026**. The network data are independent, but the difference cannot establish better performance on comparable evenings. (`LIB-REGISTER`, rows `S01`–`S09`; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §1; `LIB-NETWORK`, row `annual-all-branches`)

4. **“All ten sessions cleared the 4.5 visits-per-hour threshold.” — Unsupported and directly contradicted.** Six observed sessions met the threshold (`S01`, `S04`, `S06`, `S07`, `S08`, `S09`). Three fell below it: `S02` had 8/2 = 4.0 visits per hour, `S03` had 0/1 = 0, and `S05` had 4/1 = 4.0. `S10` is unknown because the counter was offline and no manual tally exists. The Board’s rule specifies at least 4.5 visits per staffed hour during the added hours. It does not require every session to pass. (`LIB-REGISTER`, rows `S01`–`S10`; `LIB-OUTAGE` §1; `LIB-BRIEF` §1)

**Critical corrections before a decision.** Lead with the observed result: **4.85 visits per staffed hour across 13 of 15 scheduled hours**. Whether the full pilot met the Board’s threshold depends on S10. At least 68 visits were needed across 15 hours, so S10 needed at least **five visits** beyond the 63 recorded. Assuming zero for S10 gives 4.20 visits per hour as a sensitivity calculation only. The actual full-pilot rate remains unknown. (`LIB-BRIEF` §1; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §§1–2; `LIB-REGISTER`, rows `S01`–`S10`; `LIB-OUTAGE` §1)

Remove the 18% causal conclusion and the replication claim. If the network figure is retained, identify its population and period and drop the performance judgment. Defer the permanent extension and repeat the pilot with a backup tally. The observed hours met the planning threshold, but the missing count leaves the full-pilot decision unresolved, and the study design cannot establish future demand. (`LIB-ANALYSIS` §§2–3; `LIB-PRESS` §§1–2; `LIB-VENDOR` §1; `LIB-NETWORK`, row `annual-all-branches`; `LIB-BRIEF` §§1–2)
