1. **“The ten-evening pilot measured 63 visits in 13 hours and proved the added hour caused an 18% increase.” — Partially supported.** The count is **supported** for the nine evenings with usable data. Their register entries sum to 63 visits over 13 staffed hours. The pilot scheduled 15 hours across ten evenings, and S10’s count is unknown. (`LIB-REGISTER` rows S01–S09 and S10; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §§1–2; `LIB-OUTAGE` §1)  
   The causal claim and the 18% figure are **unsupported**. The press release calls the pilot proof of an increase, and the vendor claims an 18% increase for evening programs generally. Those sources establish what each publisher said. The release supplies no new measurement, the vendor supplies no underlying data or link to this pilot, and the pilot lacks a control group, randomized schedule, or comparable pre-pilot evening series. (`LIB-PRESS` §§1–2; `LIB-VENDOR` §1; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §3)

2. **“The press release is an independent replication of the analyst’s result.” — Unsupported.** The release explicitly credits the analyst summary for its figures. The analyst calculated them from the pilot register, and the release presents no separate count. The agreement documents how the same result was reported. (`LIB-PRESS` §§1–2; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §1; `LIB-REGISTER` rows S01–S09)

3. **“The pilot outperformed the network by 21.2%.” — Misleading.** Both rates and the numerical difference are **supported**. The observed pilot rate was 63/13, or 4.85 visits per staffed hour rounded. The network rate was 1,200/300, or 4.00. Using the unrounded rates, the pilot figure was 21.2% higher. The performance inference is **unsupported**. The pilot measured nine evenings with usable counts in October 2026. The independent network figure covers all staffed hours at four branches throughout 2025. The two figures describe different populations, periods, and schedules. (`LIB-REGISTER` rows S01–S09; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §1; `LIB-NETWORK` row `annual-all-branches`)

4. **“All ten sessions cleared the 4.5 visits-per-hour threshold.” — Unsupported.** Three observed sessions directly contradict the claim. S02 recorded 8 visits in 2 hours (4.0 per hour), S03 recorded 0 in 1 hour, and S05 recorded 4 in 1 hour. Six observed sessions met the threshold (S01, S04, S06, S07, S08, S09). S10 cannot be classified because its counter was offline and no manual tally exists. (`LIB-BRIEF` §1; `LIB-REGISTER` rows S01–S10; `LIB-OUTAGE` §1)

**Critical corrections before a decision.** Report the observed result as **4.85 visits per staffed hour across nine counted evenings**. That exceeds the board’s 4.5 planning threshold for the observed hours. Whether the full pilot met it depends on S10. Across 15 scheduled hours, the pilot needed at least 68 visits, so S10 needed at least 5. Assuming zero visits for S10 would yield 63/15 = 4.20, but the outage record establishes only that the count is unknown. (`LIB-BRIEF` §1; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §§1–2; `LIB-OUTAGE` §1)

Remove the 18% causal claim, the replication claim, and the network performance conclusion. Replace the universal threshold claim with the six met, three missed, and one unknown session results. For the permanent-hours decision, repeat the pilot with a backup tally before relying on the threshold. The observed rate is encouraging, but the full-pilot rate remains unresolved and the study design does not establish future demand. (`LIB-REGISTER` rows S01–S10; `LIB-ANALYSIS` §§2–3; `LIB-BRIEF` §§1–2)
