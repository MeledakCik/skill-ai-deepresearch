**Corrected headline**  
The district’s estimated literacy rate among residents aged 15 or older was 84% in 2025, compared with 82% in 2024, while 91% of assessed literacy-program participants passed the program’s reading test in 2025. (`statistics-report.md`, §3 “Population estimate”; `education-report.md`, §4 “Completion assessment”)

**The official figures do not conflict.** The household survey estimates the share of all district residents aged 15 or older who can read a short everyday passage. The program figure counts 273 passes among 300 voluntarily enrolled adults who completed its end-of-program test. The draft’s 9-point claim comes from subtracting the 2024 population estimate from the 2025 program pass rate, which measures neither group’s change over time. (`draft-headline.md`, §1; `statistics-report.md`, §3; `education-report.md`, §4)

**What remains unresolved.** The survey used the same sampling frame and field method in both years, so its point estimates support a like-for-like increase of **2 percentage points**. The reported 95% intervals are 79–85% for 2024 and 81–87% for 2025. The report gives no formal test of the difference, so the packet leaves open whether population literacy actually increased. It also leaves the campaign’s effect unresolved. The program report has no control group, and neither report estimates a causal effect. (`statistics-report.md`, §3; `education-report.md`, §4)
