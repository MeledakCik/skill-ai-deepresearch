**Corrected headline:** The district household survey estimated that 84% of residents aged 15 or older could read a short everyday passage in 2025, up from an estimated 82% in 2024, while 91% of assessed participants in the voluntary literacy program (273 of 300) passed its end-of-program reading test. (`statistics-report.md` §3 “Population estimate” and `education-report.md` §4 “Completion assessment”)

The official figures are compatible. The survey estimates literacy across the district, while the program pass rate describes only participating adults who completed the assessment. Subtracting the 2024 survey estimate of 82% from the 2025 program pass rate of 91% mixes populations and measures, so it cannot support the nine-point increase claimed in the draft. (`draft-headline.md` §1, `statistics-report.md` §3 and `education-report.md` §4)

**What remains unresolved.** The survey used the same sampling frame and field method both years, so its estimates support a two-percentage-point rise in the point estimate. The 95% intervals were 79–85% in 2024 and 81–87% in 2025. Their overlap alone cannot determine whether the change was statistically significant, and the survey reports no formal test of the difference. Whether district literacy truly rose therefore remains uncertain. The campaign’s contribution also remains unknown because the program had no control group and neither report estimated a causal effect. (`statistics-report.md` §3 and `education-report.md` §4)
